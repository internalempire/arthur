# The Campbell diagram

> The Campbell panel plots pleural pressure against absolute lung volume. It shows the passive recoil of the lung and chest wall, the pressure generated by the respiratory muscles and the volume traversed during each breath.

---

## The clinical idea

The lung tends to recoil inward. At ordinary resting volumes, the relaxed chest wall tends to expand outward. The volume at which these opposing forces balance is the relaxation volume, **Vrel**. In a passive subject without external PEEP, intrinsic PEEP or gas trapping, Vrel corresponds approximately to functional residual capacity (FRC).

The diagram places **pleural pressure**, $P_{pl}$, on the horizontal axis and **absolute lung volume** on the vertical axis. Oesophageal pressure is commonly used as a bedside surrogate for pleural pressure. The two static curves are:

- $C_L$, the lung pressure–volume relation, plotted in the reverse direction as $-P_L$, where $P_L$ is transpulmonary pressure;
- $C_{cw}$, the relaxed chest-wall pressure–volume relation, plotted as chest-wall recoil pressure $P_{cw}$.

The curves lean in opposite directions and meet at Vrel. The label becomes **FRC = Vrel** only when the actual end-expiratory state can reasonably be identified with passive relaxation.

## What the labels mean

The notation $C_L$ and $C_{cw}$ is familiar clinical shorthand for lung and chest-wall compliance. Strictly, compliance is not the entire drawn curve: it is the local change in volume produced by a change in pressure,

$$
C = \frac{\Delta V}{\Delta P}
$$

and therefore corresponds to the magnitude of the local slope of each pressure–volume relation. Because both relations are curved, compliance changes with operating volume. With pressure on the horizontal axis and volume on the vertical axis, a more vertical segment represents greater compliance; a flatter segment represents lower compliance.

## How to read the panel

- The **purple dashed $C_L$ curve** shows inward lung recoil as $-P_L$.
- The **green dotted $C_{cw}$ curve** shows the pressure exerted by the relaxed chest wall at each volume.
- The **bright green trajectory** is the current $P_{pl}$-volume breath. The preceding breath remains faintly visible for comparison.
- The horizontal Vrel line marks the passive balance point between lung and chest wall.
- The live dot marks the current pleural pressure and lung volume.

During spontaneous inspiration, pleural pressure becomes more negative than the pressure predicted by the relaxed chest wall at the same volume. The horizontal distance between the live point and $C_{cw}$ is inspiratory muscle pressure:

$$
P_{mus} = P_{cw}(V) - P_{pl}
$$

The panel marks this distance with an orange dashed segment. Clinically, this is the pressure being generated to move the respiratory system away from passive relaxation.

During passive mechanical ventilation, inspiratory muscle pressure is absent. The pleural-pressure trajectory is therefore determined predominantly by the chest wall and may overlap $C_{cw}$. Ventilator pressure is represented by airway and alveolar pressure in the [waveforms](panel-waveforms.md), rather than as an additional Campbell curve.

## Zooming the diagram

The `+` and `−` controls enlarge or reduce the view around the current pleural-pressure–volume point. Both axes are magnified so that the tidal trajectory and its distance from the passive curves remain readable. The centre is fixed when zoom is activated: the axes do not chase the moving point during the breath.

The middle button reports the selected magnification. **Fit** restores the complete static construction. Zoom changes only the view; it does not alter respiratory mechanics, ventilator settings or model state. Selecting a scenario or changing a control recentres the magnified view on the new operating point.

## What happens during expiration

When respiratory muscles are inactive, pleural pressure follows the relaxed chest-wall relation:

$$
P_{pl} = P_{cw}(V)
$$

This does **not** mean that airway resistance has disappeared. Alveolar pressure remains the sum of chest-wall and lung recoil, while flow depends on the gradient between airway and alveolar pressure:

$$
P_{alv} = P_{cw}(V) + P_L(V)
$$

$$
\dot V = \frac{P_{aw} - P_{alv}}{R_{aw}}
$$

Airway resistance therefore changes how quickly volume returns toward equilibrium. With high resistance or expiratory flow limitation, expiration may remain incomplete, producing dynamic hyperinflation and intrinsic PEEP. The model does not include a separate viscoelastic resistance of lung tissue or chest wall; its resistive contribution is concentrated in the airways.

## How the model constructs the curves

The static curves use the same lung and chest-wall functions as the real-time simulation.

The $C_{cw}$ relation is mechanically independent of the lung. `Chest wall compliance` sets its local slope around the reference region, while `Chest wall load` changes the pressure required to hold a given volume. The relation bends toward the volume extremes rather than remaining linear indefinitely.

The $C_L$ relation is obtained by sweeping transpulmonary pressure through the model's lung pressure–volume function and plotting the resulting volume against $-P_L$. `Aerated lung compliance`, maximum lung capacity and the open lung fraction determine its position and shape. When recruitment hysteresis is active, opening and closing branches are displayed separately. Their separation represents recruitment-state memory, not the complete hysteresis of a measured human respiratory system.

The fitted axes cover the static pressure–volume domain of the selected patient. They are determined by the model parameters rather than by the instantaneous tidal excursion.

## Limits

- Pleural pressure is known exactly in the simulation. Real oesophageal-pressure measurements require positioning, calibration and awareness of regional pleural-pressure gradients.
- One aggregate chest wall replaces separate rib-cage, abdominal and diaphragmatic compartments.
- The model does not include tissue viscoelasticity, chest-wall resistance or a separate active expiratory-muscle pressure.
- Surface-tension and tissue hysteresis are absent. When two $C_L$ branches are visible, their separation describes recruitment memory only.
- Vrel equals FRC only in a passive system without external or intrinsic PEEP.
- The displayed $P_{mus}$ is the pressure generated by the model's inspiratory-effort mechanism. It is not a measurement of diaphragmatic pressure, pressure–time product or metabolic work.
