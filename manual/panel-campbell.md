# The Campbell diagram

> The Campbell panel places airway, alveolar and pleural pressure against lung volume so the elastic and resistive pressure components of a breath can be separated.

---

## Physiology

At any lung volume, pleural pressure describes the pressure applied to the chest wall, while transpulmonary pressure—the difference between alveolar and pleural pressure—distends the lung. Airway pressure differs from alveolar pressure while gas is flowing because part of the applied pressure is spent across airway resistance.

On a pressure–volume diagram, the distance between relaxation relations represents elastic pressure; separation between dynamic airway and alveolar paths represents resistive pressure. In a spontaneously breathing patient, respiratory muscle pressure contributes to the work and changes how the diagram is conventionally partitioned.

## How to read the panel

Pressure is on the horizontal axis and volume above the resting reference on the vertical axis. Bright lines are the current breath and faded lines are the preceding breath.

- **Ppl** follows the chest-wall side of the breath.
- **Palv** sits between pleural and airway pressure and reveals the pressure required to move the elastic system.
- **Paw** includes the additional pressure lost across airway resistance while flow exists.
- **Ccw**, **Clung** and **Crs** label the chest-wall, lung and combined respiratory-system relaxation relations.

In passive volume control, the expiratory Paw limb can be nearly vertical: external PEEP is held constant while volume falls. This is expected from the ventilator representation, not a physiological assertion that alveolar pressure is constant during expiration; Palv traces the dynamic expiratory relation.

## In the model

The lung relaxation line is generated by the same nonlinear pressure–volume function used by the respiratory integrator. In a recruitable lung it therefore develops a lower inflection. With recruitment hysteresis active, separate opening and closing branches show retained recruitment of the diseased population.

The dashed chest-wall relation is also generated from the same independent sigmoid used by the integrator. It is almost straight around tidal breathing, but its slope changes toward the volume extremes. Changing lung compliance, collapse or maximum lung capacity moves the lung relation and the passive intersection; it does **not** translate the wall curve. `Chest wall load` shifts the wall relation, whereas `Chest wall compliance` changes its local slope.

The combined respiratory-system relation is the sum of lung and wall recoil at each absolute volume. This is why the three curves continue to agree with the live Paw, Palv and Ppl points even when the resting volume changes.

The vertical scale is chosen once from the current ventilator settings and the model's lung–chest-wall mechanics. It remains fixed throughout the breath, so an apparent change in loop height reflects a change in volume rather than a moving axis. Changing a control or selecting a scenario recalculates the scale once for the new conditions.

The area between branches is not a complete measured respiratory-system hysteresis loop. Tissue viscoelasticity and surfactant hysteresis are absent, so the panel shows recruitment-state memory only. See [hysteresis](hysteresis.md).

## Why this construction

Plotting Paw alone would mix elastic and resistive pressure. Adding Palv and Ppl makes the route from ventilator pressure to lung distension and thoracic pressure visible, which is the part relevant to cardiovascular interaction.

## Limits

- One global lung and one aggregate nonlinear chest wall replace regional mechanics, pleural gradients and separate rib-cage/diaphragm behaviour.
- The diagram is reconstructed from exact internal pressure and volume, without oesophageal-pressure artefact or calibration error.
- Respiratory muscle work is not partitioned into pressure–time product or oxygen cost.
- Surface-tension and tissue hysteresis are absent; the displayed recruitment loop is not the complete classical lung P–V loop.
- Routine plateau pressure is calculated rather than measured during an end-inspiratory occlusion.
- The fixed scale is a display envelope derived from the selected settings. It is not a predicted tidal-volume measurement.
